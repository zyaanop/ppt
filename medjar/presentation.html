Multi-Agent Clinical Reasoning · Research Seminar

MedJar: Consensus Diagnosis by Debating Specialist Agents

Persona-conditioned language-model specialists reason independently over a patient case, ground every claim in retrieved literature, and are driven toward a calibrated, auditable diagnosis through structured debate.

MedJar Working Group
Clinical Machine Learning · Decision-Support Systems
Technical report and working prototype · assistive, human-in-the-loop · not an autonomous diagnostic device
Motivation

Diagnostic error is a systems problem

An estimated 5% of adults experience a diagnostic error in outpatient care each year, concentrated in complex presentations that cross specialty boundaries.

  • Anchoring — an initial impression suppresses competing hypotheses.
  • Premature closure — the search ends once a plausible answer appears.
  • Specialty siloing — the experts who would resolve the case never reason on it together.
  • Knowledge drift — guidelines change faster than anyone tracks them.

Medicine already has a countermeasure — the tumour board. What makes it work is not consensus but structured disagreement. Can that be made computational?

Motivation

Why a single model is insufficient

Conditioning one model on “you are an expert physician” collapses the diversity of clinical reasoning into a single distribution.

Correlated failure

One prior, one blind spot. No internal adversary to surface the missed diagnosis.

Miscalibrated confidence

Self-consistent but wrong trajectories, asserted at high confidence.

Unverifiable grounding

Claims not bound to a source cannot be checked, audited, or contested.

Majority voting does not fix this: it discards why agents differ and systematically silences the minority can’t-miss diagnosis — the most consequential error mode in acute care.

Contributions

Contributions

  1. Specialist multi-agent formulation. Distinct reasoning priors with private, specialty-scoped retrieval, so the diversity debate consumes is preserved rather than averaged away.
  2. A structured debate protocol operating on grounded reasons, converting disagreement into targeted retrieval.
  3. A consensus formalism — confidence-weighted aggregation, evidence adjustment, and a disagreement metric driving an explicit stopping and escalation rule.
  4. A competence-scoped critique rule which we show is necessary: without it, agents lacking domain priors vote down correct in-domain diagnoses.
  5. A complete deterministic prototype and an evaluation protocol for the system as decision support — with negative results reported.

Method

Grounding, the debate protocol, and the consensus formalism.

Method · Grounding

Hybrid retrieval, then a hard grounding gate

Figure 4. Dense recall and BM25 exact-term matching are merged by reciprocal rank fusion, re-ranked for precision, then gated: a claim not entailed by its cited passage is excluded from aggregation.
Method · Agents

The specialist ensemble

Each agent is a triple ⟨persona, tools, retrieval scope⟩. Scoped retrieval keeps perspectives distinct — global retrieval would homogenise the ensemble.

AgentReasoning priorCharacteristic question
Radiologistmorphology → localisation → imaging differentialWhat does the image show, independent of the referral question?
Cardiologistexclude life-threatening cardiac aetiology firstIs there a cardiac cause or consequence we must not miss?
Oncologisttissue of origin, staging, biomarkersIs this neoplastic, and what is the stage-defining evidence?
Generalistwhole-patient coherenceDoes this explain the entire presentation?

Reasoning contract. Every turn returns structured output — per-hypothesis likelihood, supporting and refuting claims bound to citation ids, the discriminating test, red flags, confidence — never free prose. Agents never abstain: an agent with no prior for a hypothesis registers a low cautionary floor, which counts in S(h) but not in D̄.

Method · Debate

Propose → critique → rebut → assess

Figure 5. Proposal is isolated to prevent anchoring; critique is anonymised; contested points seed targeted re-retrieval. At least one critique round always runs — coinciding impressions are still cross-examined. Labels abridged from the prototype’s CASE-001 transcript.
Method · Consensus

Consensus formalism

S(h) = Σᵢ wᵢ(h)·cᵢ·pᵢ(h)Σᵢ wᵢ(h)·cᵢ(1) confidence- and competence-weighted mean
S*(h) = σ( α·logit S(h) + β·E(h) )(2) evidence adjustment
Disagree(h) = Σi∈𝒮 wᵢcᵢ( pᵢ(h) − S(h) )²Σi∈𝒮 wᵢcᵢ(3) weighted variance

wᵢ(h) — soft MoE

1.00 in-domain, 0.58–0.82 adjacent, 0.45 otherwise. Not a router: every agent votes on every hypothesis.

E(h) — evidence

Support minus refutation, weighted by evidence grade, recency and entailment.

𝒮(h) — substantive only

Floors are excluded from (3): absence of an opinion is not dissent.

Method · Consensus

From agent opinions to S*(h) and D̄

Figure 6. Values are the prototype’s actual round-0 likelihoods for primary lung malignancy in CASE-001. The cardiologist’s 0.17 is a cautionary floor, not a considered dissent.
Method · Control

Deterministic orchestration

Figure 7. States and guards. τ_agree = 0.010, τ_flag = 0.30, R_max = 4, r_min = 1.
  • Control flow is a finite-state machine, not a model — predictable, testable, auditable.
  • Converge: D̄ ≤ τ_agree → aggregate and report.
  • Budget: r = R_max → escalate with the open disagreement stated.
  • Can’t-miss: any red-flag dx with S* ≥ τ_flag → escalate, overriding consensus.
  • The override is asymmetric: it can force escalation, never suppress it.
Method · Output

The report leads with uncertainty

Figure 8. Status, can’t-miss panel and disagreement precede the differential.
  • Ordering is a safety decision, not a stylistic one.
  • The clinically valuable content is what the system is unsure about.
  • Points of disagreement are surfaced, never suppressed — they mark where judgement is needed.
  • Every claim carries a citation into the Evidence Ledger; the debate is replayable.
Method · Deployment

Trust boundaries

Figure 9. Identifiers and raw pixels stay in the PHI enclave; the reasoning zone sees only the de-identified CCO; re-identification happens solely at the point of care, through a restricted token service, and is logged.

  • CASE-002 falls below τ_agree (0.0112 → 0.0104 → 0.0093) and converges.
  • CASE-001 plateaus above it (0.0384 → 0.0345 → 0.0345 → 0.0345 → 0.0357): the specialists narrow but cannot reconcile, so the case escalates with its open disagreement stated.
  • CASE-003 agrees almost immediately yet still escalates — a can’t-miss diagnosis overrides consensus.
  • D̄ gives a principled, compute-bounded criterion for “we are not sure — ask a human.”
Results

Why competence weighting matters

Figure 11. CASE-001, final round. Real run data.
  • On the leading hypothesis the agents split 0.83 / 0.17 / 0.90 / 0.79.
  • The cardiologist’s 0.17 is a cautionary floor, not a considered dissent — it has no oncology prior.
  • Specialty weighting stops it dominating S*(h), and it is excluded from D̄ entirely.
  • Without both rules, ignorance would masquerade as disagreement.
Results

Evidence adjustment does real work

Figure 12. CASE-001 differential. Real run data.
  • The gap between S and S* is the contribution of grounded evidence.
  • Primary lung malignancy gains: E = +0.72, S 0.72 → S* 0.79.
  • Malignant pericardial effusion loses: E = −0.34, 0.37 → 0.33 — the retrieved guidance states cytological confirmation is required, so the corpus argues against asserting it.
  • Plausibility and support are separated.
Results

Calibration: a negative result

Figure 13. Real run data; small-sample, not a validation result.
  • ECE = 0.243 over 19 hypothesis-level predictions — poor.
  • Temperatures and α, β are hand-set, not fitted on held-out data.
  • Eq. (1) weights agents by their confidence, so this requirement is currently unmet.
  • S* should be read ordinally, not as a probability. Fitting the calibration layer is the first prerequisite for any performance claim.
Findings

Four failure modes the implementation exposed

(a) Hypotheses floating on their prior

A diagnosis whose supporting features were entirely absent still scored ≈0.33 on its prior intercept alone — putting lung malignancy top of the differential for a patient with no nodule. Fix: zero matched features ⇒ penalised out of contention.

(b) Incompetent critique → false reassurance

Three agents with no cardiology competence critiqued the cardiologist’s correct ACS position using their own ignorance floor as the comparison. S* fell 0.84 → 0.27, below τ_flag, and the case converged instead of escalating — manufacturing exactly the false reassurance the system exists to prevent. Fix: the right to object is tied to competence.

(c) Unbounded reinforcement → echo chamber

Uncontested positions inflated every round, so apparent disagreement drifted upward — the opposite of the intended dynamic. Fix: reinforcement capped at the agent’s own prior. Consolidation may restore a position; it may not inflate it.

(d) Ignorance floors counted as dissent

Floors never move, so including them in Eq. (3) created irreducible disagreement: the benign case exhausted its budget instead of converging. Fix: restrict (3) to substantive opinions.

Aggregation rules, not agent count, determine whether an ensemble is safe. An unweighted debate among heterogeneous agents can be more dangerous than one competent agent — it gives confident non-experts a mechanism to overrule a correct specialist.

Evaluation

The study this is not (yet)

Figure 14. Expected effects — hypotheses to be tested, not measurements.
  • Data: knowledge probes; clinicopathological-conference cases; a prospective silent trial, read-only, never influencing care.
  • Primary endpoint is safety, not accuracy: can’t-miss recall and the false-reassurance rate.
  • Also: ECE / Brier, groundedness, blinded specialist ratings, rounds-to-converge, and subgroup parity.
  • Ablations: single agent · majority vote · debate without RAG · without calibration · full system.
Safety

Assistive by design — the clinician decides

Human gate

Mandatory sign-off before any recommendation informs care. Uncertainty and can’t-miss findings escalate rather than close a case.

Privacy

De-identification at intake · PHI enclave · encryption · per-access audit · no training on patient data absent governance and consent.

Regulatory

Decision support driving diagnosis is likely Software as a Medical Device. Transparent evidence and a replayable transcript support the “clinician can independently review the basis” pathway — but deployment needs clinical validation, a predetermined change-control plan, and post-market surveillance.

Equity

Corpus and evaluation stratified by subgroup, calibration checked per stratum and monitored continuously.

MedJar does not autonomously diagnose or treat, and is not a substitute for professional medical judgement.

Conclusion

Conclusion

MedJar recasts the multidisciplinary case conference as a computational protocol: specialists reason independently, ground every claim, debate under rules that tie the right to object to competence, and converge on a calibrated, auditable assessment — escalating to a human precisely when they should.

Demonstrated

The full pipeline runs end to end, deterministically, and D̄ works as a control signal across all three stopping paths.

The transferable result

Naive multi-agent debate is not automatically safer. We watched a correct can’t-miss diagnosis suppressed by agents with no competence in the domain.

Next

Fit the calibration layer; then a prospective silent trial — the only way to learn whether any of this helps a clinician.

Aggregation rules, not agent count, determine whether an ensemble is safe.
