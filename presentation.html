Multi-Agent Clinical Reasoning · Research Seminar

MedJar: Consensus Diagnosis by Debating Specialist Agents

A framework in which persona-conditioned language-model specialists independently reason over a patient case, ground their claims in retrieved literature, and are driven toward a calibrated, auditable diagnosis through structured debate.

MedJar Working Group
Clinical Machine Learning · Decision-Support Systems
Seminar presentation · assistive, human-in-the-loop · not an autonomous diagnostic device
Motivation

Diagnostic error is a systems problem

An estimated ~5% of adults experience a diagnostic error in outpatient care each year; harm concentrates in complex, multi-system presentations that cross specialty boundaries.[1]

  • Anchoring & premature closure — the differential collapses before the evidence is exhausted.
  • Specialty siloing — the relevant experts rarely reason on the case simultaneously.
  • Knowledge drift — guidelines change faster than any individual tracks them.
Motivation

Why a single "expert" model is not enough

Conditioning one model on "you are an expert physician" collapses the diversity of clinical reasoning into a single distribution. It cannot represent the productive tension between specialties, and its chain-of-thought is prone to self-consistent-but-wrong trajectories with poorly calibrated confidence.

Correlated failure

One prior → one blind spot. No internal adversary to surface the missed diagnosis.

Overconfidence

Fluent assertion is uncorrelated with correctness; confidence is miscalibrated.

Opaque grounding

Claims are not tied to a citable source, so they cannot be checked or audited.

Our thesis: recover the value of a multidisciplinary team by making specialist models disagree productively and reconcile against evidence.

Contributions

Contributions

  1. Specialist multi-agent formulation. Persona-conditioned agents with distinct reasoning priors and private, scoped retrieval, preserving the perspective diversity that debate exploits.
  2. A structured debate protocol (propose → critique → rebut → converge) that operates on reasons, not just answers, and treats disagreement as a control signal.
  3. Confidence-weighted, evidence-adjusted consensus with a formal disagreement metric and an explicit stopping / human-escalation rule.
  4. Grounding & calibration. Every claim is verified against a retrieved passage; reported confidence is post-hoc calibrated.
  5. An evaluation protocol for the system as decision-support: accuracy, calibration, can't-miss safety, groundedness, and human utility.

Part II
II

Method

Agents, grounding, the debate protocol, and the consensus formalism.

Method · Agents

Specialist agents

Each agent is a triple ⟨persona, tools, retrieval scope⟩. Personas encode distinct reasoning priors; scoped retrieval keeps their perspectives distinct — global retrieval would homogenize the ensemble and destroy the diversity debate depends on.

R

Radiologist

Reasons from image morphology and localization before the referral hypothesis. Scope: imaging atlases, appropriateness criteria.

C

Cardiologist

Prioritizes exclusion of life-threatening cardiac etiologies. Scope: ESC/ACC guidelines, risk scores, ECG libraries.

O

Oncologist

Assesses tissue of origin, stage-defining evidence, actionable biomarkers. Scope: staging manuals, oncology guidelines.

Reasoning contract. Every turn returns structured output — a differential with per-hypothesis likelihood, supporting and refuting evidence keyed to citation IDs, a discriminating next test, red-flags, and calibrated confidence — never free prose. This makes positions comparable, aggregable, and auditable. A generalist agent is always present to represent the whole patient; the ensemble is a plug-in registry.

Method · Grounding

Retrieval-augmented grounding

The corpus is versioned with effective dates and evidence grades, so recency and strength of evidence are first-class.

Method · Debate

The debate protocol

Debate, not voting

Majority voting discards why agents disagree and silences the minority can't-miss diagnosis. Debate operates on grounded reasons.

Disagreement as signal

Points of contention determine what is retrieved next and whether the case must escalate.

Method · Consensus

Consensus formalization

Over the shared hypothesis space h ∈ H, agent i reports likelihood pi(h) with calibrated confidence ci and a soft specialty weight wi(h) (higher inside its domain — a soft mixture of experts, not a hard router).

Ensemble score   S(h) = Σi wi(h)·ci·pi(h)Σi wi(h)·ci (1)
Evidence adjustment   S*(h) = σ( α · logit S(h) + β · E(h) ) (2)

E(h) = weighted (supporting − refuting) grounded citations, so higher-grade, more recent evidence is rewarded; σ is the logistic function.

Confidences ci are post-hoc calibrated (temperature / Platt scaling on a held-out set) so that stated confidence matches empirical accuracy — a prerequisite for trustworthy weighting.

Method · Control

Disagreement as a control signal

Disagree(h) = Σi wi(h)·ci·( pi(h) − S(h) )2Σi wi(h)·ci (3)

A confidence-weighted variance across agents; the case-level signal D̄ is its mean over the top-k hypotheses.

Stopping rule — the debate halts when any of:

convergeD̄ ≤ τagreeaggregate via Eqs. (1)–(2)
boundr = Rmaxescalate with open disagreement
red-flagany can't-miss dx > τflagimmediate human escalation

Aggregation rewards grounded evidence, not rhetoric — an eloquent but unsupported argument cannot win.

Method · Orchestration

Deterministic orchestration

Control flow is a deterministic state machine — not a free-form LLM — so it is predictable, testable, and auditable.

Evaluation

Protocol, and illustrative results for accuracy, calibration, and convergence.

Evaluation · Protocol

Experimental protocol

Data

  • Knowledge probes: MedQA (USMLE), MedMCQA, PubMedQA.
  • Case-based: clinicopathological-conference cases with confirmed final diagnoses; MIMIC-derived scenarios.
  • Prospective silent trial: read-only, alongside real workups, no influence on care.

Baselines & ablations

  • Single specialist agent; majority-vote ensemble.
  • MedJar without RAG; without calibration.
  • Full MedJar (debate + scoped RAG + calibration).

Metrics

AccuracyTop-1 / Top-3 differential hit rate
CalibrationECE, Brier score, reliability diagram
Safetycan't-miss recall; false-reassurance rate (primary)
Grounding% claims entailed by cited passage
Utilityblinded specialist ratings
Costlatency, tokens, rounds-to-converge

Evaluation · Results

Ablation: each component contributes

  • Ensembling (vote) adds over a single agent, but plateaus — it ignores why agents differ.
  • Structured debate adds more by reconciling reasons.
  • Scoped RAG yields the largest single gain and suppresses hallucination.
  • The full system compounds these effects.
Evaluation · Results

Calibration matters for trust

Raw model confidence sits below the diagonal — overconfident. Temperature / Platt scaling on held-out data pulls the curve toward the identity line.

Only calibrated confidences can be trusted as the weights ci in Eq. (1). We report ECE and Brier alongside accuracy.

Evaluation · Results

Disagreement drives the stopping decision

Most cases converge within a few rounds (Case A). When agents cannot reconcile (Case B), D̄ stays above τ and the case is escalated with the open disagreement made explicit — the safety-preserving default.

This gives a principled, compute-bounded criterion for "we are not sure — ask a human."

Evaluation · Case study

Worked case

58-year-old: progressive dyspnea, weight loss, spiculated right-upper-lobe nodule on CT, mildly elevated troponin, small pericardial effusion. Synthetic

Radiologist

Spiculated nodule → primary lung malignancy leads; recommends PET-CT and biopsy; notes effusion.

Cardiologist

Troponin + effusion → exclude ACS and tamponade physiology first; troponin may be demand ischemia.

Oncologist

Malignant effusion would upstage — but requires cytologic confirmation, not assumption.

Reconciliation

Debate converges on the discriminating tests — tissue biopsy + effusion cytology; residual disagreement remains on stage.

Red-flag → escalate

Tamponade is the can't-miss. The report ships with an explicit open question — hemodynamic compromise? — for urgent human review.

Discussion

Safety, limitations, and honest failure modes

Safeguards

  • Mandatory clinician sign-off; the system recommends, never acts.
  • Uncertainty and red-flags escalate rather than close a case.
  • HIPAA/GDPR de-identification; PHI enclave; full access audit.
  • Likely regulated as SaMD; transparent evidence supports independent review.

Where it can fail — and mitigation

Incomplete case filesescalation gate
Correlated errors (shared base)heterogeneous models + devil's advocate
Corpus stalenessversioned, evidence-graded curation
Persuasive-but-wrongevidence-weighted, not rhetoric-weighted
Clinician automation biaslead with uncertainty & disagreement

MedJar is decision-support. It does not autonomously diagnose or treat, and is not a substitute for professional medical judgment.

Conclusion

Conclusion

MedJar operationalizes the multidisciplinary case conference as a computational protocol: specialist agents that reason independently, ground every claim, debate their differences, and converge on a calibrated, auditable diagnosis — escalating to a human exactly when they should.

Grounded & transparent

No claim without a checkable citation; the full debate is replayable.

Calibrated & safe

Confidence is trustworthy; disagreement and red-flags route to humans.

Assistive by design

The clinician remains the decision-maker and is accountable for care.

Next: multimodal ingestion and calibration → prospective silent trial → regulated, human-in-the-loop pilot.
